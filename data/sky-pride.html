What does it take to climb out of the trash and into the sky?

The very heavens had decreed his death. He should have died in the womb. Or in infancy. Or when his family was murdered. Or when the house burned around him. Or when he woke up in the dump suffering from innumerable diseases and missing fingers. Even the magic ring with a ghostly grandpa trapped inside of it was sabotaged. A reasonable person would roll over and die. Tian isn't that reasonable. And as it happens, neither is Grandpa Jun. In a world where immortality can be cultivated, miracles are things you make for yourself. A broken boy will defy the heavens, and soar.

What to expect:

-1 chapter a day M-F. Volume 3 is already complete.

-2k words per chapter (aprox, some run long) with a longer first chapter.

-No Harem

-Earned power growth

-An MC who isn't a goodie two shoes OR an edge-lord psychopath

-A power system based on actual Daoism 

-Sects, giant monsters, natural treasures, flying swords, spells, talismans, secret arts, and a gentle sprinkling of young masters whose face need rearranging.

Excellent story of growth

Reviewed at: Chapter 46- Waking Up
by pVegie Monday, April 14, 2025 4:03:33 PM

An excellently told xain-xia story with a protagonist that isn't a self insert and a self awareness of the genre to not make it boring. Tian is ignorant, having been sick, sheltered and then left for dead as a small child. What sets him apart from other "Ignorant" main characters is his ability to acknowledge his ignorance and grow in a realistic manner. Tian isn't dumb, he is merely used to being alone and learned different things. His lessons are hard fought victories for survival, trial and error, failure comes often, but it teaches him.

His "Grandfather" isn't a "Golden Finger" or a system granting power for just breathing, but a personality with their own agenda and harsh limits. This gives agency to Tian's personal growth while also helping to guide him in the best direction.

Well written characters and a welcome lack of "Young Master-itis" give this story enough of a difference to stand out in a choked genre. That being said, it is a well trod path being xain-xia and there is currently nothing game changing enough in the setting to truly make this a 5 out of 5. Give it a read and you will be most pleased.

Extremely fun! And inequal ?

Reviewed at: Chapter 37- The Gentle Art of Taking A Hit
by Zabzablord Tuesday, June 10, 2025 3:46:44 PM

This review has been edited in light of volume 2 being well on its way to an end. Sky Pride remains a difficult object to assess.

The writing is definitely the selling point. I want to insist once again that this has the catching power of a funk song. I don't know how you could not dance along. Is it a fun read? Absolutely, indubitably yes! Do not doubt that for a second.

I still think volume 1 is inconsistant in its tones, to the point it becomes jarring. As if the author doesn't really know what story they want to tell, it goes from comically absurd to soberingly dramatic.

The characters fielded are archetypal, I haven't changed my mind. For volume 1 at least. They do not work against the story, no. They mesh rather well, in fact. But they aren't that deep - think, basically, shonen protag. Please do note that I'm not saying this negatively. Volume 1 story does work wonderfully with its cast.

I still feel most encounters (as in, fights, because we're talking xianxia with a shonen protag) of volume 1 lack that little something that would make them truly great. I think, mostly, that it comes down to suspense. You don't fear for Tian for a second. And you don't doubt he is going to triumph from the challenges.

Alright. Volume 2?

Let me say it outright, volume 2 on its own get the full five stars. Writing is still catchy as hell. Most importantly, it's absolutely consistant in tone: that of a bleak war story where hope, though tenuous, remains shining nonetheless.

The characters get layers. Remember the shonen protag? Well, you can forget him. I'm actually unsure what Tian will conclude from all he has learned and I'm here for it. And the author works in sufficiently subtle ways, meaning it's not about him turning evil or anything boring like that, but about ethics, ways to live one's life, and so on. Very much yes.

I actually came to fear for the life of a supporting character, and I'm holding my breath everytime the absentee mentor is mentioned. Suffice to say, suspense has been reintroduced full force.

Go through book 1 (it's not that bumpy of a ride) and enjoy book 2.

This story follows a kid who is ostensibly very, very sickly, maimed, and amnesiac enough to have forgotten his six-year noble upbringing - and still somehow figured out hunting and trapping from first principles.

And, you know, watching someone figure out something that complicated, even from a much easier starting point, can be pretty compelling! But it actually happened offscreen. The author does seem to have a decent joke or two, and a remarkably good update rate. But given that this is their second major work on this site, I suspect they aren't improving so fast that I'll like their next one. Maybe whatever they're working on in ten years.
